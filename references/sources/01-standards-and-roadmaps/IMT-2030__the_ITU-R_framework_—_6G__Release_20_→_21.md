### IMT-2030: the _ITU-R_ framework

3GPP does not decide what 6G must achieve — the ITU-R does, in a framework called IMT-2030. This chapter reads that framework as an engineer: six usage scenarios, fifteen capabilities, and the exact way 3GPP turns them into requirements.

#### 2.1 Who decides what a generation is?

Two organisations, two jobs. The **International Telecommunication Union Radiocommunication Sector (ITU-R)** is the UN body that governs spectrum and defines the _International Mobile Telecommunications (IMT)_ family. Inside it, **Working Party 5D** owns IMT. It does not write radio protocols. It writes the _framework_ , the _minimum requirements_ , the _evaluation method_ , and finally the _recommendation_ that says which technologies qualify. Each generation has had an IMT name: IMT-2000 (3G), IMT-Advanced (4G), **IMT-2020 (5G)** , and now **IMT-2030 (6G)** . The name is the ITU-R's; "5G" and "6G" are the industry's. FIG 2.1 The ITU-R process that produces a generation Vision Recommendation M.2160 (Nov 2023) Requirements Technical performance requirements report Evaluation method Evaluation guidelines report Proposals 3GPP and others submit candidate technologies Evaluation Independent evaluators test the candidates Recommendation IMT-2030 radio specification, around 2030 ITU timeline The ITU-R sets the target; it does not write the protocol. 3GPP's Release 21 is 3GPP's candidate for IMT-2030, in the same way that Release 15 and 16 were its candidate for IMT-2020. Exact ITU-R dates are planned by WP 5D and can shift. Expected The consequence for this book is simple and important: **every number in a 3GPP 6G document traces back to an ITU-R document** , or is explicitly marked as a 3GPP-internal target. TR 38.914 says so in its first KPI clause: its targets "contain KPI targets from Report ITU-R M.[IMT-2030. TECH PERF REQ] and 3GPP specific requirements; both shall be supported". When you read a 6G KPI, ask first: _is this an ITU-R requirement or a 3GPP choice?_

#### 2.2 Six usage scenarios

IMT-2020 had three usage scenarios: eMBB, URLLC and mMTC. M.2160 keeps three of them — each broadened and renamed — and adds three more. TR 22.870 adopted the same six as the structure of its whole use-case study.

Scenario| Relationship to 5G| In plain words  
---|---|---  
**Immersive Communication**|  Extends eMBB| Very high rates plus new media: extended reality, holographic and multi-sensory experiences, with guaranteed experience  
**Hyper Reliable and Low-Latency Communication (HRLLC)**|  Extends URLLC| Tighter latency and reliability for control loops: industrial, vehicular, remote operation  
**Massive Communication**|  Extends mMTC| Very many devices, including more capable ones than today's sensors  
**Ubiquitous Connectivity**|  New| Reach places today's grid does not: rural, remote, maritime, indoors, through satellites and platforms  
**Artificial Intelligence and Communication**|  New| The network supports distributed computing and AI applications — and uses AI itself  
**Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC)**|  New| The network senses the physical world — position, speed, shape of objects, connected or not  
  
FIG 2.2 Six scenarios: three extended, three new Immersive Communication extends eMBB Hyper Reliable, Low-Latency extends URLLC Massive Communication extends mMTC Ubiquitous Connectivity new — coverage, satellites, resilience NEW AI and Communication new — compute and learning in the network NEW Sensing (ISAC) new — the network as a radar NEW Blue = evolved from IMT-2020. Gold with a NEW tag = added by IMT-2030. Chapters 26–29 treat each of these in depth.

##### 2.2.1 What the new three mean for the standard

The first three are _performance_ scenarios: the same kind of service, more of it. The new three are _capability_ scenarios: they add a service the 5G system was never asked to provide. That is why the architecture work in Part IV needs new functions (sensing, AI agents, compute), and why the radio needs new measurements and signals (Chapter 27).

#### 2.3 The overarching aspects

Besides scenarios and capabilities, M.2160 names aspects that cut across everything: **sustainability** , **connecting the unconnected** (universal, affordable access), **ubiquitous intelligence** , and **security and resilience** , together with standardisation and interoperability. TR 22.870 echoes the same principles in its overview and TR 38.914 turns them into requirements: energy efficiency (clause 5.1.15), resilience and extended connectivity (5.1.19), and security and privacy (5.3.7). _·_ Why "sustainability" is a requirement, not a slogan In TR 38.914 clause 5.1.15, energy efficiency is defined as the _relative_ energy consumption at a chosen load compared with a fully loaded reference. The 6G radio must support low relative consumption at least at **zero load** and at one partial load (0 % < L ≤ 30 %). A radio that idles efficiently is now part of the definition of compliance.

#### 2.5 From an ITU range to a 3GPP number

If M.2160 says 50 to 200 Gbit/s, why does TR 38.914 say 36 Gbit/s? Three reasons, each worth learning because the same pattern recurs for every KPI. **1\. Framework versus requirement.** M.2160 is aspirational and scenario-dependent. The _minimum requirement_ for a candidate technology is lower, and is what a proposal must demonstrate. TR 38.914 clause 5.1.1 defines peak data rate as bandwidth times peak spectral efficiency, and then derives its number: 60 bit/s/Hz (DL) × 600 MHz of aggregated bandwidth = 36 Gbit/s; 30 bit/s/Hz (UL) × 600 MHz = 18 Gbit/s. It adds that "the assumed bandwidth is not part of this requirement" — the requirement is the spectral efficiency; the bandwidth only makes it a headline. R_peak = W × SE_peak DL: 600 MHz × 60 bit/s/Hz = 36 Gbit/s UL: 600 MHz × 30 bit/s/Hz = 18 Gbit/s (IMT-2020 for comparison: 30 bit/s/Hz DL gives 20 Gbit/s, which implies about 667 MHz of aggregation — simple arithmetic, [[Analysis]]) **2\. Practicality of a single radio.** A range like "50 to 200 Gbit/s" includes sub-THz and very wide bandwidths that a first 6G release aimed at frequencies from 410 MHz to 52.6 GHz (Chapter 16) will not address. 3GPP anchors its target to what its radio can credibly evaluate. **3\. 3GPP adds its own KPIs.** TR 38.914 contains KPIs that M.2160 does not: the **coverage** KPI (MaxCL 143 dB, with 153 dB for extended coverage), the **airplane** mobility class (500 to 1 000/1 200 km/h), and **vertical positioning** accuracy. Each is labelled "3GPP internal KPI" in the text. _◆_ Operator view — reading a vendor 6G claim When a vendor states a 6G figure, ask which of four things it is: an ITU-R aspiration (M.2160), an ITU-R minimum requirement, a 3GPP target (TR 38.914), or a proprietary lab result. They are not interchangeable, and only the second and third are obligations on the eventual standard.

#### 2.6 Definitions you will need for Part II

The KPIs are defined carefully in TR 38.914 clause 5.1, and later chapters depend on the wording. A short glossary: * **Peak data rate** — the maximum achievable rate under ideal conditions, error-free, for a single device using all assignable resources, excluding synchronisation, reference signals, guard bands and guard times. * **5th-percentile user data rate** — the 5 % point of the user-throughput distribution: what the worst-served 5 % of users get. * **Average spectral efficiency** — aggregate throughput of all users divided by system bandwidth and number of TRxPs, in bit/s/Hz/TRxP. * **Area traffic capacity** — total throughput per unit area, in Mbit/s/m², equal to TRxP density × bandwidth × average spectral efficiency. * **User-plane latency** — one-way radio-network delay for a small packet in an unloaded network. * **Connection density** — devices per km² that meet a specified quality of service. * **Mobility interruption time** — the shortest period during which a device cannot exchange user-plane packets during a transition. The formula in the definition of area traffic capacity is the most useful in practice: doubling the number of sites, the bandwidth or the spectral efficiency each doubles capacity. 6G's 40 Mbit/s/m² indoor-hotspot target (four times IMT-2020's 10) is reached by all three levers at once. C_area = ρ × W × SE_avg ρ = TRxP density (per m²) W = system bandwidth SE = average spectral efficiency (bit/s/Hz/TRxP)

#### 2.7 How 3GPP reports back to the ITU-R

The approved TR 38.914 was announced by 3GPP as a contribution to the ITU-R IMT-2030 process; the study "can now be shared with ITU-R". From here the flow is one-directional until 2027–2029: 3GPP keeps refining its candidate in Release 21, and submits it with an evaluation showing it meets the IMT-2030 requirements, using the ITU-R evaluation guideline (the TR says the evaluation methodology of Report ITU-R M.[IMT-2030.EVAL] "shall be used"). The ITU-R then publishes its recommendation, conventionally around 2030, at which point the technology is formally "IMT-2030". 5G vs 6G **IMT-2020 versus IMT-2030 at the framework level**

    *  It lists fifteen capabilities, nine enhanced from IMT-2020 and six new, with indicative target ranges rather than single numbers.
    *  3GPP's TR 38.914 translates the framework into KPI targets and adds 3GPP-internal KPIs; its numbers are a subset of, and sometimes lower than, the aspirational ranges.
    *  3GPP contributes its technology to ITU-R as a candidate; ITU-R evaluates it against the requirements and publishes the IMT-2030 specification around 2030.
    
[/code]

##### Review questions
[code] 
    1. Which three IMT-2030 usage scenarios are new compared with IMT-2020? Answer Ubiquitous Connectivity, Artificial Intelligence and Communication, and Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC).
    1. How many capabilities does M.2160 define, and how are they split between old and new? Answer Fifteen: nine are enhancements of IMT-2020 capabilities and six are new (coverage, positioning, sensing-related, AI-related, sustainability and interoperability).
    1. TR 38.914 gives 36 Gbit/s DL peak rate; M.2160 cites a range of 50 to 200 Gbit/s. Give one reason the numbers differ. Answer M.2160 gives indicative aspirational ranges for the framework; the binding minimum requirements come from the technical-performance-requirements report, and 3GPP's 36 Gbit/s is derived from 60 bit/s/Hz peak spectral efficiency over an assumed 600 MHz aggregate.
    1. What is a '3GPP internal KPI'? Answer A target 3GPP sets for its own design that is not an ITU-R minimum requirement — for example the coverage KPI (MaxCL), vertical positioning accuracy, and the airplane mobility class in TR 38.914.
    
[/code]
