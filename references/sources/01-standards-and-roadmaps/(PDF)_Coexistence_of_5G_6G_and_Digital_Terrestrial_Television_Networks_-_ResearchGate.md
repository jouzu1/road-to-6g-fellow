Abstract This paper analyzes practical solutions for implementing 5G/6G and Digital Terrestrial Television (DTT) network coexistence in the UHF band. Two potential scenarios are explored using the case study of a nationwide DTT network: spectrum splitting and spectrum interleaving. Both scenarios are thoroughly examined based on current UHF spectrum usage and the DTT network structure based on a case study of Poland. The analysis includes potential future benefits and limitations of each scenario. The results presented in this paper can guide administrations in implementing decisions made at the International Telecommunication Union—Radiocommunication Sector, World Radiocommunication Conference 2023 (ITU-R WRC23), improving spectrum efficiency and 5G/6G networks development. Introduction Currently, 5G networks are being deployed worldwide, and many countries are in the preparation phase for new 6G networks. However, the implementation of 5G networks in many regions is gradual and limited [ 1 ]. In the coming years, 5G coverage will not extend to many geographical areas, particularly rural and remote locations where coverage is necessary. These areas beneﬁt from low-frequency bands, which offer better pr opagation conditions. The spectrum below 1 GHz (Ultra-high frequency (UHF) 300–1000 MHz range) is optimal for 5G/6G wide network coverage and in some countries, the 700 MHz band is already deployed for such purposes. However, the 700 MHz band (694–790 MHz) is quite limited (e.g., in Europe, only 2 × 30 MHz frequency-division duplex (FDD) spectrum is used) and more spectrum in the UHF band will be required for nationwide 5G/6G networks in the coming years. Due to the rapid development of wireless telecommunications and the increasing use of radio spectrum, there are growing issues r elated to the scarcity of available radio frequencies, necessitating new spectrum solutions. Spectrum sharing, where the same part of the spectrum is used for various types of communications across different radio services (e.g., mobile, ﬁxed, satellite, or broadcasting) in the same frequency bands and geographical areas, is becoming increasingly beneﬁcial. This issue is particularly signiﬁcant for low-frequency ranges (i.e., UHF bands below 1 GHz) [ 2 , 3 ], where systems beneﬁt from excellent propagation conditions and efﬁcient, small-sized antennas. Shared and interleaved spectrum usage in the same frequency range for network coexistence can be recognized and accepted at the regulatory level. Such radio spectrum management occurs at multiple levels: globally (regulated by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) Radio Regulations, applicable to all ITU member states); regionally (e.g., agreements or regulations from regional organizations such as the Eur opean Conference of Postal and T elecommunications Administrations (CEPT), the Radio Spectrum Policy Group (RSPG) in Europe [ 4 ], or the European Commission (EC)) [ 5 ]; and nationally (by national regulators Appl. Regulatory Activities on the IMT and DTT in the UHF Band Radio frequencies in any given territory are typically divided among different ra- dio services or systems. Introducing a new radio system in a band where other systems or services already operate requires thorough electr omagnetic compatibility analyses to ensure interference at acceptable levels. During the W orld Radiocommunication Con- ference (WRC) of the ITU-R in 2012 [ 9 ], the 700 MHz band (694–790 MHz range) was initially allocated to mobile services. This band, previously used almost exclusively for broadcasting worldwide (particularly in ITU Region 1, which includes Europe, the Middle East, and Africa), was given primary allocation status for 4th and 5th-generation mobile systems (referred to in ITU nomenclature as IMT—International Mobile Telecommunica- tions). As a result of this decision, WRC12 led to the development and implementation of policies on a regional scale in Europe. The EC issued Decision 2016/687 on 28 April 2016, to harmonize the 700 MHz band. Additionally, the European Parliament and Council issued a directive (COM/2016/043 ﬁnal [ 10 ]) mandating that all EU countries allocate the 700 MHz band for mobile IMT systems by 30 June 2020, with a possible derogation for up to two years. Signiﬁcantly, during WRC12, the 700 MHz band was allocated on a shared basis with digital terrestrial television (DTT). The demand for radio spectrum for IMT systems and the pressure from the telecommunications sector on individual ad- ministrations were exceptionally strong during WRC12. As a result, a shared allocation of the 700 MHz band ( 694–790 MHz ) was established, despite this band being allocated for terrestrial television purposes throughout ITU Region 1 as part of the ITU Regional Radiocommunication Conference RRC04-RRC06 [ 11 ]. As a result, only the 470–694 MHz frequency range remained exclusively available in the UHF band for digital terrestrial tele- vision (DTT) using DVB-T (Digital Video Broadcasting—T errestrial) or DVB-T2 standards in Europe and more broadly in countries covered by the ITU RRC04-RRC06 agr eement (known as Geneva 2006—GE06 [ 11 ]), including Africa, the countries of the former USSR (Union of Soviet Socialist Republics), the Middle East, and Iran. Therefore, the guaranteed frequency range for DTT in these countries under the GE06 agreement is currently the 470–694 MHz frequency range. Due to the growing telecommunications trafﬁc in 4G/5G mobile networks and the anticipated increase in requirements for 6G, both in terms of capacity and network coverage, especially in low-frequency ranges (<1 GHz), the ITU-R WRC15 conference [ 12 ] discussed allocating additional parts of the UHF band, such as the 600 MHz band, for IMT systems. However, no global allocation was made, with only a few exceptions, such as the USA and Canada, which introduced such frequency allocations locally within their territories as footnotes to the Radio Regulations. Under further pressur e from the telecommunications operators, a resolution was developed during WRC15 (Res. 235 WRC15) [ 12 ], which proposed that the entire 470–960 MHz band should be analyzed and discussed at subsequent radiocommunication conferences. During the subsequent conference (WRC19), inﬂuenced by Resolution 235 WRC15, it was decided to include an agenda item for the next conference (WRC23), concerning the possibility of allocating radio spectrum for IMT systems across the entire 470–960 MHz band in ITU Region 1. This deci- sion is in accordance with Resolution 235 WRC15, which called for the necessary analysis of the 470–694 MHz band to explore potential changes in frequency allocations during the ITU WRC23 conference (Agenda Item 1.5 of the WRC23 Agenda Items, Res. 1399 WRC19 [ 13 ]. During the preparation for WRC23, numerous electromagnetic compatibility analyses conducted within the ITU forum and research teams were pr esented [ 14 – 22 ]. It became evident that operating two such distinct services in the same frequency range—terrestrial television (DTT) and IMT mobile systems—is not feasible without several hundred kilome- ters of geographical separation or special new techniques used. Possible Solutions for IMT/DTT Coexistence Now it is necessary to obtain the results of technical analyses that allow the assessment of various approaches to this issue, particularly ensuring the continued functioning of DVB-T/T2 terrestrial television, which is widely watched and received in many countries. This paper attempts to resolve the conﬂict of access to the UHF spectrum 470–694 MHz on a “win-win” basis: to address the gr owing spectrum demand from 5G/6G systems and the desire for IMT access to the 470–694 MHz band, while also meeting the needs of maintaining the broadcast of DVB-T/T2 digital terrestrial television in the 470–694 MHz band. The paper is based on detailed analyses, using Poland as a case study. As a result of these detailed analyses, two solutions were proposed to potentially increase the availability and efﬁciency of radio spectrum use in the bands below 1 GHz for IMT systems, while ensuring appropriate DVB-T/T2 emissions: 1\. Dividing the 470–694 MHz frequency range into two parts: 602–694 MHz for IMT and 470–602 MHz for DVB-T/T2. 2\. Sharing the entire 470–694 MHz spectrum range using an interleaving spectrum concept, with IMT as a secondary service and DVB-T/T2 as the primary service, based on compatible cooperation without modifying existing TV network plans. The rest of the paper is organized as follows: network planning basis and current spectrum usage, requirements and DTT network planning conditions, allotment alloca- tion modiﬁcation results after spectrum splitting (method 1), detailed results of possible spectrum sharing and interleaving (method 2), and conclusions and practical applications. 1\. Networks Re-Planning One approach involves dividing the 470–694 MHz frequency range into two parts: allocating the 606–694 MHz range to IMT systems and dedicating the 470–606 MHz range to digital terrestrial television. This method reduces the frequency resources available for terrestrial television, currently spanning the entire 470–694 MHz range. As a result, the emission of the six nationwide multiplexes currently operating within the 470–694 MHz range (corresponding to TV channels 21–48) would need to shift to a narrower band of 470–606 MHz (channels 21–37). This shift would entail a loss of 11 TV channels (equivalent to 88 MHz of spectrum) and necessitate signiﬁcant adjustments to the plans of TV networks and the organization of frequency utilization. Speciﬁcally, it would require enlarging the size of frequency allotment areas for individual channels to ensure system compatibility and avoid interference between multiplexes, following GE06 frequency planning rules and detailed guidelines for DTT networks. The analysis used Poland as a representative example within Europe. It is assumed that any necessary changes would also prompt regional re-planning at least within the EU or even more br oadly across CEPT countries, neighbors, or the entire GE06 region. The objective of this planning exercise was to assess the feasibility of consolidating all six nationwide DVB-T2 multiplexes within a signiﬁcantly constrained frequency band of 470–606 MHz across the entire country, thereby freeing up the remaining spectrum (606–694 MHz) for IMT use. The decision to allocate the upper portion of the 470–694 MHz band for IMT stemmed from the reservation of the 600 MHz (617–698 MHz) band in certain countries (e.g., the USA) for IMT purposes, which aligns with the existing allocation within this range, such as the n71 3GPP band (referred to as the US digital dividend band). The analysis of DVB-T/T2 networks necessitates the application of a suitable and in- ternationally accepted calculation method, which serves not only for domestic veriﬁcation Appl. Sci. 2024, 14, 5756 4 of 16 but also for business analyses conducted by operators and broadcasters. The methodology employed for determining the coverage and design of terrestrial television networks was developed by the authors at the IT Systems of the National Institute of T elecommuni- cations as part of their earlier work, primarily catering to commercial requirements. It adheres to international standards and recommendations [ 11 , 23 , 24 ], relying on analytical techniques tailored for single-frequency network (SFN) analysis and design [ 25 ], as well as implemented propagation models [ 26 ] that utilize digital elevation maps and databases encompassing both existing and planned TV stations. In this paper 's analyses, the most optimal DVB-T2 operating modes were applied to maintain the bit rate at the current level (a minimum of 36 Mb/s) and to minimize intra-network interference across very large SFN. Speciﬁcally, the chosen parameters include 256QAM modulation, 32k-extended transmission mode, a guard interval (GI) of 19/128, a code rate (CR) of 3/4, pilot pattern 2 (PP2), and an 8 MHz channel bandwidth. The usable signal for DVB-T2 stations was quantiﬁed based on a statistical threshold that ensures coverage exceeding 50% of the time in 95% of locations near the receiving point under stationary reception conditions (using a directional antenna at a height of 10 m above ground level). For the case of the interfering signal, it was characterized by the ﬁeld strength exceeded in 50% of locations and 1% of the time. The analysis also considered the network gain of the SFN and its impact on the resulting usable area, as well as the SFN self-interference affecting the network, utilizing the k-LNM method [ 25 ]. For ﬁeld strength calculations, the propagation curves outlined in ITU-R P.1546-6 [ 26 ] were employed. Discussion The paper examines the feasibility of re-purposing the sub-1 GHz UHF band in the range 470–694 MHz, at present designated for digital terrestrial television, for use by IMT 5G/6G and other future telecommunications systems. The analyses suggest that it is viable to split the 470–694 MHz spectrum, designating the 606–694 MHz segment for IMT systems, while preserving the 470–606 MHz for exclusive DTT use. Alternatively, the second method shows that the entire 470–694 MHz range could be intelligently shared on a supplementary downlink basis for 5G/6G IMT services. Both approaches to accessing the valuable sub-1 GHz UHF band offer a strategy to secure additional spectrum for IMT/5G/6G systems without compromising the integrity of prevailing and necessary DTT services. If the sub-1 GHz UHF band were divided to create dedicated portions for 5G/6G IMT and DTT services, a considerable replanning of all terrestrial television multiplex net- works would be required. This scenario, exempliﬁed by the case study presented, would necessitate collaboration with neighboring countries, a process currently under discussion at the European level and pending potential decisions at future ITU-R radiocommunica- tion conferences. While reallocating the spectrum would involve signiﬁcant frequency planning and co-ordinations issues, modiﬁcations, and expenses for network operators, redistribution would yield the considerable advantage of making the entire 600 MHz band ( 606–694 MHz ) available for both uplink and downlink 5G/6G IMT operations. How- ever, this option could impose stricter spectr um constraints on terrestrial television and is likely to present substantial challenges for international coordination, complicating the introduction of additional transmissions, such as local television programs. International coordination of such a solution can take 3–5 years and must include any neighboring administrations in the region that wish to adopt it. Because DTT usage is dropping in certain countries, their administrations may be not interested in coordinating efforts for a system that might be turned off in the next few years, despite their desire to implement IMT across the entire DTT spectrum. T aking into account the terms of reference of the ITU WRC31, where the UHF band discussion may resume and a discussion about DTT turn-off may occur, such a temporary solution of spectr um splitting may be unacceptable to the administration, leaving the solution as an interesting but unexplored idea. The option ne- cessitates extensive administrative efforts as well as numerous DTT network operator tasks, resulting in additional costs and effort for network operators and broadcasters, and may be viewed as a costly solution for achieving duplex mode transmission in the low-UHF band. Depending on the speciﬁc location, as demonstrated by the results of the case study in Poland, the interleaved spectrum available for SDL IMT 5G/6G mode could range from 16 MHz (two 8 MHz TV channels) to as much as 160 MHz (twenty 8 MHz channels). These substantial bandwidths are particularly attractive as they can be introduced without interfering with current existing terrestrial network operations, thereby avoiding the costly and labor-intensive restructuring associated with DTT network replanning (as is the case with the ﬁrst proposed method). Such an advanced scheme would not only dramatically increase the 5G/6G networks' downlink range and capacity but would also circumvent the extensive process of international channel coordination and the high expenses tied to the modiﬁcation of DTT infrastructure. This aligns with the ITU-R WRC23's endorsement of assigning the 470–694 MHz frequency range to IMT as a secondary service. The only disadvantage of the solution is limiting the free spectrum for additional broadcasting services in the band: e.g., additional local TV stations or Service Ancillary to Broadcasting (radio-microphone, cameras, etc.). Nevertheless, these services may be also transferred to IMT systems through the interleaved IMT spectrum, for instance, by employing 5G broadcast mode. Intelligent sharing across the entire 470–694 MHz range is feasible in downlink mode, enabling a generous allocation of spectrum for broadcasts from base stations to users, which typically face the highest demand. Implementing this solution necessitates sophisticated ra- dio planning and the utilization of cognitive radio allocation strategies, such as geolocation databases [ 30 ] or a light licensing regimen [ 31 ]. While it obliges network operators to adopt [](<https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381939780_Coexistence_of_5G6G_and_Digital_Terrestrial_Television_Networks#pf10>) [](<https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381939780_Coexistence_of_5G6G_and_Digital_Terrestrial_Television_Networks#pf10>) Appl. Sci. 2024, 14, 5756 14 of 16 innovative spectrum management techniques, this method spares the DTT networks from any expensive alterations and minimizes the need for public engagement that typically accompanies TV channel reallocation. Intelligent spectrum sharing minimizes further modiﬁcation requirements of DTT networks by assigning additional spectrum to new IMT services. This second method has a signiﬁcant beneﬁt over the ﬁrst since it can be deployed instantly, unlike the ﬁrst way, which requires time-consuming frequency coor dination procedures. Given the possibility of switching off DTT in some countries, the ﬁrst method may only be a temporary solution, taking into account terms of reference of the ITU WRC31, where the UHF band discussion will return and possible discussion about DTT turn-off may occur, requiring a signiﬁcant administrative ef fort for a short period of time. The second method has the advantage of being easily transferable to a full UHF band IMT solution, with IMT uplink acceptable at the next stage, and does not necessitate additional international administrative coordination efforts or costly DTT network changes. 1\. Conclusions This paper has outlined potential challenges to and solutions for the seamless integra- tion of emerging IMT 5G/6G systems with existing DTT networks within the 470–694 MHz UHF frequency band. The proposed solutions are designed in order to protect existing DTT broadcasting while facilitating the evolution of DTT networks and concurrently expanding frequency resources for the IMT services. The effective implementation of these solutions would lead to a mutually beneﬁcial scenario for both broadcasting and mobile telecom- munications. The solution presented, drawing on a case study conducted in Poland, can be easily applied in other countries within the ITU GE06 Agreement area (Europe, Africa, the Middle East, Iran, and former USSR countries), since the same frequency management regulations, techniques, and practical DTT implementations are used. For countries with long-term DTT exploitation plans, both alternatives are appealing. The ﬁrst requires more administrative effort and engagement from adjacent countries and costly DTT network modiﬁcations, but it allows achieving a full duplex (uplink–downlink) IMT mode in a relatively short time (within a few years). However, for countries that want to switch off DTT in the next years, the ﬁrst solution may be deemed unworthy of signiﬁcant effort; hence, the second option will be most appealing. The feasibility of these approaches will rely on the choices made by countries, con- sidering the current outcomes of the ITU-R WRC23 conference [ 32 ], which validate the potential for secondary utilization of the IMT systems within the DTT UHF frequency range in numerous countries. Regardless of the countries' decisions, the technical examination outlined in the paper provides valuable perspectives for national regulators and interested parties to contemplate for ensuring future proof and effective frequency management and spectrum efﬁciency. This is crucial for fostering the coexistence of 5G/6G services with DTT networks in the UHF band, ensuring that spectrum allocation advancements keep pace with technological growth and societal needs.
