Sigit Arifianto This paper proposes a novel design of polar codes for rateless transmissions employing extended parity (EP) to enhance performance under broadband channel conditions. The idea of the proposed design is to achieve diversity across all samples by employing simple butterfly XOR operations, which inherently support rateless broadband transmissions. In particular, the design exploits the principle of repetition, where simple XOR operations do not only contribute to error protection but also strengthen the polarization effect and reinforce the rateless property of polar codes. The proposed codes are evaluated over Rayleigh fading, fully interleaved, and additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channels. The results show that the proposed codes achieve significant performance improvements, particularly in AWGN and fully interleaved environments, thereby confirming that the use of XOR operations effectively enhances transmission reliability. Furthermore, the proposed codes are investigated through extrinsic information transfer (EXIT) analysis using closed-form expressions. The analysis reveals that the decoding process exhibits faster convergence when EP is employed. In addition, computational complexity analysis shows that the additional overhead introduced by EP remains minimal. Importantly, the proposed structure preserves the standard polar transform and decoding graph, ensuring scalability similar to conventional polar codes. Hence, the proposed design balances performance and computational efficiency, making it a compelling solution for broadband scenarios and dynamic channel environments. [Quadrature NOMA-ALOHA: Throughput Bounds](<https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393737789_Quadrature_NOMA-ALOHA_Throughput_Bounds>) [Article](<https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393737789_Quadrature_NOMA-ALOHA_Throughput_Bounds>) * Jan 2025 * IEEE Wireless Communications Letters * I Nyoman Apraz Ramatryana * Gelar Budiman * Gandeva Bayu Satrya * [...] * Sobia Jangsher This letter studies cross-layer physical (PHY) and medium access control (MAC) contention-based random access for uplink non-orthogonal multiple access assisted by slotted ALOHA (NOMA-ALOHA). In addition, an improved NOMA-ALOHA is proposed by exploiting signal space diversity using π/4-rotated M-ary amplitude shift keying (MASK) with coordinate interleaving (CI), known as quadrature NOMA-ALOHA (QNOMA-ALOHA). In QNOMA-ALOHA, the PHY layer creates a new transmission resource based on two quadrature carriers (cosine and sine) and generates a virtual subslot in the MAC layer. The throughput bounds of QNOMA-ALOHA are derived based on the Poisson model. The numerical results show that the improved NOMA-ALOHA with QNOMA in the PHY layer significantly outperforms the existing NOMA-ALOHA in terms of throughput. The improvement of QNOMA-ALOHA is two times the square root of the power level to slotted ALOHA and two times to NOMA-ALOHA.
